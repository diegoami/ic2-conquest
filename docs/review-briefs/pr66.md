## Blocking means (any one is enough; a blocking finding means rework, never approve)
1. A Done-when line of `docs/tasks/cosmetic-gaps.md` fails, or cannot be run as written by a reviewer.
2. What this task protects can be got past: a rule stated as exact whose literal, number or trigger does not match its cited
   source line; a `[confirmed]` tag on a claim that has no recording, screenshot or save behind it; a `[derived]`/`[confirmed]`
   mix inside one claim; a caption or title literal retyped from memory instead of read from the dump or the form resource.
3. Behaviour the task forbids: a play claim without its screenshot and save; a staged save not labelled as staged; a measured
   output overwritten or deleted (a re-run must write new files, never replace); a binary committed to git; a write to another
   repository; a click at a guessed or fixed position (every click of every recording must carry its proof: the tooltip-proved
   x, the `win_state` control line, or the driver's tile targeting).
4. Project-specific: the claims audit (`runs/experiments/cosmetic_gaps/claims_audit.py`, output
   `runs/experiments/data/run-exp-cosmetic-gaps/claims_audit_cosmetic.v3.txt`) must really re-read its sources — a bent claim
   (change one byte of a caption, one case line, one play tag) must produce mismatches (`test_claims_audit.py` proves each);
   a test that passes with the behaviour deleted is blocking; a finding claim with no tracked output behind it.
   The audit and test outputs must be the tracked, versioned ones, not the reviewer's own re-typing.
Not blocking: wording, style, and defects in code the PR did not change. File those as follow-ups.
When unsure, rate it blocking and say why. An approve with a proven bypass is the costliest mistake a review can make.
