## 1. The hook

`patches/d734tail_hook.py` is observation-only. It logs:
- every entry of the AI walk's end check `FUN_0044d734` (0x44D737);
- each of the three actions its tail can take, with the arguments, the army's position, supplies and money:
  - `F6D8` resupply at a city that is not at war (0x44D935, row C23);
  - siege (0x44D949);
  - attack (0x44D99B).

It also logs the return chain: `D734`'s own return, and the first caller of the `FUN_0044dba8` chain that is not
`DBA8`'s own recursion (v2 of the hook).

Tests: `tests/test_d734tail_hook.py` runs every probe under Unicorn on the real exe: 7 of 7.

**Addresses.** Call sites are the address of the `call` instruction (for example `F31C` 0x44F47C). The tables'
caller columns and `summary.json` list the **return address** the hook logs, which is the call site + 5 (0x44F481).

Runs: the 16 civ-sweep starts, 5 rounds each, seed 12345, the human giving no orders (the layout of `run-exp-econ2`). The
runner is `difftest.probes.fleetphase_hook_run`.
- **v2** (hook v1, outer return one frame up): the hooked autosaves are byte-identical to the plain build's in 16/16 starts.
- **v3 + v3b** were meant to run hook v2 (walking past `DBA8`'s recursion). They reused the hooked exe built for v2: the
  runner built it only when the file was missing (fixed: it now rebuilds whenever the module's build differs). Their
  logs are identical to v2's in 16 of 16 starts, so the counts below **reproduce exactly**. v3 stopped after S07
  when a second Wine job collided with it (13:04, my scheduling error); v3b ran S08–S15.
- **v4**, with hook v2, is scheduled between two long-game queue items, and attributes the recursion bucket (§3).
- The first attempt (`c22c23-hook-v1`) failed at start, before any record. My Wine-hold placeholder was named
  "Imperial Conquest 2 hold", and the driver's `pgrep -f "^Imperial Conquest"` picked it; it is now named "hold: …".

## 2. C23: the walk-end resupply exists and is common, but changed nothing measurable

In v2 (16 × 5 rounds), `D734` was entered 1,525 times. Its tail made:

| tail | count | callers (the `DBA8` chain's caller in `F31C`, from the disassembly) |
|---|---|---|
| **resupply (`F6D8`)** | **571** | 306 at the resupply walk (`F31C` 0x44F47C: support test failed or no city target → march to `E670`'s city); 258 under `DBA8` recursion (v3 attributes them, §3); 4 `E84C`; 3 `EBE8` garrison |
| siege | 49 | 21 city target (0x44F43F), 15 after `E84C` (0x44F4C7), 10 recursion, 3 `EBE8` |
| attack | 64 | 20 capital defence (`EFC8`), 17 army target (0x44F409), 27 recursion |

The land-tail recursion (0x44D8E5, row C24) never fired. No entry came from the human order path (0x446DB1/0x446DEA).

**The remake has no such resupply.** Its "move to the resupply city" march (`AiMilitaryPhase.cs:469`) carries no end
check (`EndCheck` None). Its `OriginalArmyWalkContact` City check only proposes a siege, which is illegal at a city not at
war. So C23 stays **MISSING in code**.

**The effect in state.** `c23_supply_effect.py` replays each hooked round on the remake from the original's own round
start, and compares the armies that got a resupply tail at the end of the round:
- **The units compared.** There are 323 distinct (round, army, city) tails. The army is compared in 294; 29 are skipped
  because the remake's record index does not hold the same nation. The city is compared in all 323, since cities keep
  their index.
- **army supplies:** equal in 293 of 294 (1 higher in the remake) (`c23-supply-v1.rows.jsonl`);
- **army money:** equal in 294 of 294 (`c23-money-stock-v1/`, added at research intake PR #213, which noted the figure
  was not in the data);
- **the tail city's stock:** equal in 321 of 323 (same file). The 2 exceptions (Numidia city 330: 579 vs 531; Dacia city
  120: 193 vs 192) are not traced.

The reason is in the hook's pre-tail values. The army typically enters the tail already full (for example 377 supplies,
600 money, recurring across armies and rounds). `E41C`, the support pass at the army's entry, has just filled it from
the same city. So the tail moves nothing in this sample.

**Row C23:** MISSING (ii, iii), with no measurable effect in 80 rounds (iv). It would matter only where the support pass
leaves the army short and the walk brings it next to a city with stock.

## 4. The own-capital stays: diplomacy draws, not capital defence

The 10 own-capital rows of the v0.9.3/v0.9.4 rechecks are armies next to their own capital that the original keeps and
the remake moves. Greece t21 is X2 (`2026-11-05-x2-support-pass-timing.md`), which leaves 9 rows in 8 starts.

**Exact starts.** The v0.9.0–v0.9.2 games logged no RandSeed per checkpoint. `stays_hook_run.sh` therefore replayed each
game's original side from its start (seed 12345, rulebot-1, `IC2_LOG_RANDSEED=1`) up to the row. Every replayed save is
byte-identical to the stored game (`regen-check.json`: 38, 101, 107, 31, 27, 7, 14 and 7 saves, 0 differ), so each start
was played with the original's own RandSeed.

**The original side** (`decide_hook`, `capdef_hook`; autosaves identical to the plain build, 16/16):
- In all 9 rows the army has 7–9 moves, **no city target and no army target** (`city_score` and `army_score` both
  −10,000).
- Its resupply city (`E670`, "home") is its own capital, one tile away.
- `F31C` then walks it to the resupply city (0x44F47C). That is a one-tile walk whose `D734` tail resupplies at the
  capital (§2), so the army stays.
- Capital defence logged nothing for these nations: none was at war.

**The remake side** (anchored replays at `168dbc04`; AI logs of 3 rows in `stays-remake-ailog-v1`, and of all 9 in
`stays-remake-ailog-v2`, `stays_remake_ailog.py`):
- In every row the nation **declares war, or forms an alliance, in that seat**, and the army marches at the new enemy:
  - Seleucid declares war on Armenia and Media, and army-3 approaches a target army 84 tiles away;
  - Ptolemaic declares war on Carthage, and army-7 approaches a target army. It also ends the seat at war with Numidia,
    which is not in its own log;
  - Macedonia allies with Illyria, and army-8 approaches a target city. Its war with Greece appears only in the end
    state, not in its own log.
- The log says why: "FUN_0044FB7C's own war-target search picked it, and the Random(10) roll hit".

| row | original: new wars / alliances | remake: new wars / alliances (end state, whoever declared) |
|---|---|---|
| Rome t10, Macedonia | none | war Greece; ally Illyria |
| Carthage t33, Ptolemaic | none | war Carthage, Numidia |
| Carthage t35, Ptolemaic | none | war Carthage; ally Celtiberia |
| Gaul t29, Seleucid | none | war Armenia, Media |
| Ptolemaic t9, Rome | none | war Illyria |
| Numidia t6, Media | none | war Seleucid |
| Numidia t11, Media | none | war Seleucid |
| Numidia t11, Seleucid | none | war Armenia, Media |
| Greece t6, Carthage | none | war Rome |

None of the nine nations was at war before the seat.

**All 9 remake logs (v2).**
- In 7 rows, the nation's own `FB7C` search picked a target and its roll hit: wars by Ptolemaic (t33), Seleucid (Gaul
  t29), Rome, Seleucid (Numidia t11) and Carthage; alliances by Macedonia and Ptolemaic (t35).
- In the 2 Media rows (Numidia t6 and t11), Media's own log has no diplomacy line. Seleucid's own `FB7C` picked
  **Armenia** ("declare war on armenia ... the Random(10) roll hit"). Media was Armenia's ally (relation 2 before the turn),
  so the declaration extends to it: the turn's output prints "Seleucid declares war on Armenia." and then "Seleucid
  declares war on Media.". This holds even over the Seleucid–Media peace cooldown (−8 at t6, −7 at t11). Corrected at
  research intake PR #214; the first text said Seleucid declared on Media.
- So all 9 rows go back to an `FB7C` roll that hit in the remake: 7 the nation's own, 2 through an ally.

**Attribution: diplomacy draws, the leading reading.** The intake (research PR #213) narrowed this: `FB7C` was not
hooked on the original side, so "the original's roll missed" is inferred, and a difference in the war-target search is
not excluded. `patches/fb7c_hook.py` (Unicorn tests 4/4) logs, per seat, the search's target, a hit Random(10) war roll
and an alliance formed. Its run on the 8 exact starts is scheduled with v4 (`between_games_hooks.sh`).
- The original's war or alliance roll missed in these seats, and the remake's hit. The two games draw from different
  random streams (rows A19/F31, owner-approved APPR).
- The direction (9 of 9 "remake declares") comes from how the rows were selected: "original stays, remake moves".
- The base rate is balanced: in the X1 scan, 109 war starts were in the original only against 105 in the remake only
  (`2026-11-05-x1-war-before-economy.md`).
- This agrees with pass 1, which found every one of these rows draw-sensitive in the remake.

**What it settles:** the 9 rows are not a capital-defence or garrison rule difference (capital defence is silent, and
the original's armies have no target). Whether the original's search found the same targets is for the `FB7C` run.
