## B03. Mobilisation at a city with a hostile army adjacent: DIFF, now hit in play (iv)

**The original.** In the original, mobilisation ends in `FUN_004504f4` (a) → `FUN_0044a4e0`:
- `FUN_0044a4e0` joins the slot to an own army near the city (`FUN_0044a120`: distance 1, or < 6 for a nation without
  the `+0x490` flag), or places a new army (`FUN_00449f08`).
- `FUN_00449f08` takes the last tile of the city's 3×3 block whose unit-map code is between 2 and 11
  (`FUN_004492c0`, mode 1). It fails only when there is no such tile, or when 198 army records already exist.
- **No step tests for an enemy army.**

**The remake.** `AiEconomyPhase.ProposeMobilization` skips a city when `view.IsThreatened(city)` (a hostile army within
`ThreatenedCityAdjacencyRadius`). Its own comment says: "This restraint is the AI's own [designed] one (the original's
FUN_004504f4 has no such test)". Faithful reaches it through `AiFaithfulEconomy.cs:113`.

**In play.** `b03_threatened_mobilise.py` finds the ready slots that the original mobilised at a city with an army of a
nation at war with its owner within one tile. It found 11, in 2 seats, all at Erivan (city 297, (262,34)), Armenia, in
`thracia-rulebot1-40t`, with Seleucid's army at (263,33):
- **t38:** 7 slots, 41,500 men;
- **t39:** 4 slots, 14,800 men.

In both seats:
- **The original** mobilises the slots into a new army beside the enemy. The news gains "Seleucid destroys army of
  Armenia": the count is 1 → 2 at t38 and 2 → 3 at t39.
- **The remake** mobilises none of them and loses no army (the count stays at 1 and 2).

**The remake's refusal.** A t38 replay with the AI log (`thracia-t38-ailog`, seed 12383) shows the cause:
- Armenia's Faithful economy has threat 67,717 against own 0, and 13 ready slots open the gate, yet nothing is
  mobilised. The 13 are counted in the start save (`t37-a00.SAV`, slots at state 24); the AI log does not print them.
- Of `ProposeMobilization`'s refusals, the army cap is excluded: there are 28 army records against 198.
- The placement cell was not probed. The original placed an army from the same state.
- So `IsThreatened` is the leading cause, not a proven one: it stays so until `PlacementCell` is probed for Erivan (research intake PR #211).

**Row B03:** DIFF (authored restraint under Faithful), now **iv**, 2 seats. The effect in play: the original sacrifices
fresh recruits beside a besieging army, and the remake keeps them.

## D05. The busy build-city test counts any nation's fleet: DIFF in code, never reached

**The rules.**
- **Original:** `4496E0` counts only the builder's own fleet under construction.
- **Remake:** `AiFaithfulEconomy.cs:647` skips a city when **any** fleet under construction has
  `BuildCityId == city.Id`.
- They differ only when a city is captured while another nation's fleet is under construction there.

**The scan.** `d05_foreign_build.py` uses the build city at fleet `+20` while the countdown is ≥ 0 (`build_city` in
`state/sav.py`). Across 25 games, 2,398 original saves and 181 distinct builds, **no fleet's build city was ever owned by
another nation**. A rerun with a summary line (research intake PR #211) gives 2,416 saves, the queue's running game having grown, with the same 181 builds and 0 foreign build cities (`summary-v2.txt`).

The first version of the scan looked for the fleet on the city's tile. A fleet under construction stands at (0,0), so
that version could not find anything. Its empty output is kept as `foreign_build-blind-v0.jsonl`.

**Row D05:** DIFF (ii), **not reached in the stored games**. It needs a staged capture during construction.

## D09. Leftover moves at launch: MATCH for the launch step; the order step stays DIFF (ii)

**The original.** A fleet ordered into a reused, compacted record keeps the old fleet's leftover moves through
construction and launch, so it can sail on its launch turn (`2026-10-31-sav-export-record-lifecycle37.md`, §moves +0xC).

**Launch step: MATCH (iv).** The remake's launch (`FleetTickSystem.cs:94`) resets the position, condition, supplies and
money, but **not the moves**. `d09_launch_moves.py` lists the 91 launches during anchored AI phases:
- **6 had leftover moves** (17–31). In all 6 the remake ends with the original's tile and moves, including the fleets
  that sailed on the launch turn. For example, `rome-rulebot1-150t-h1` t25: fleet 3 goes 29 → 5 at (189,93) in both, and
  t78: fleet 6 goes 31 → 0 at (131,56) in both.
- **84 had none**, and all 84 match.
- **1 is a fleet-id difference.** At `illyria-rulebot1-40t` t18 the script finds no remake fleet with id 2 (`remake_end` is None), and the remake's fleet 1 stands at the original's tile with its moves, (219,55,0). The remake numbered the record differently; the position and moves match.

**Order step: DIFF (ii), not measurable by anchored replays.** `OrderFleetCommandHandler.cs:76` creates every new fleet
with `Moves: 0`. In an anchored replay, every fleet under construction is imported from the original's records, leftover
included, so the remake's own order never reaches a launch. That needs a free-running remake game, or a staged one
ordered into a known reused index.
